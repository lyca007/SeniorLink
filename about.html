About the project

SeniorLink: digital support and services for seniors

A large-text, simple website that brings trusted services, health reminders, community activities and family check-ins into one place.

The problem

  • Many seniors feel isolated, especially when family lives far away.
  • Services such as nurses, medicine and rides are hard to find and hard to trust.
  • Most websites use small text and confusing menus that seniors find difficult to use.

The solution

A large-text, simple website with four parts: trusted services, health reminders, community activities and a family check-in. Every screen has one clear job, and help is always one tap away.

What makes it different

One-click help button

Always visible. One press alerts family, a barangay volunteer and the senior center.

Voice navigation

Speak instead of type, for seniors with poor eyesight or shaky hands.

Verified providers

Senior centers and local government offices check each provider before it is listed.

Family dashboard

Families see daily check-ins and reminders, and can send a quick message.

Feasibility

  • A simple interface that needs no training to start.
  • Partnerships with senior centers and local government units.
  • Community volunteers, such as students, help manage listings and visit seniors.

Business model

  • Service provider listings. Verified providers pay to be listed.
  • Community donations. Individuals and groups support free features for seniors.
  • Government partnerships. Local governments fund the platform as a public service.

Seniors and families never pay to use the site.

Expected impact

Less isolation

Daily check-ins and local activities keep seniors connected.

Safer access to services

Verified providers lower the risk of scams and poor care.

A smaller generation gap

Young volunteers teach, and seniors share their stories.
